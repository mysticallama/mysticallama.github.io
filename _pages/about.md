# The seam between the people who build frontier AI and the people who write the rules for it

Hi, I'm Kevin. I work on AI strategy, with particular attention to how frontier capabilities are reshaping strategic relationships in the Pacific and how the U.S. is navigating these issues on the security end. I'm a Master in Public Policy candidate at the Yale Jackson School of Global Affairs, affiliated with the Schmidt Program on Artificial Intelligence, Emerging Technologies, and National Power, and I serve as Editor-in-Chief of the *Yale Journal of International Affairs*.

Most of my research sits at a seam that tends to be left unattended — between the people who build frontier systems and the people who write rules for them. I came to policy from the technical side, after several years as a machine learning engineer and data scientist in the U.S. national security community, and I try to write for readers on both sides of that divide.

→ The [Teaching page](/teaching/) covers the Yale courses I help teach.

## What am I up to now?

- At the [Centre for the Governance of AI](https://www.governance.ai/), I'm a Summer Research Fellow measuring how reliably auditor models detect misaligned behavior as interaction horizons lengthen, benchmarked across frontier and open-weight architectures — which I'm writing up for a NeurIPS workshop.
- I'm co-authoring a policy paper with researchers at [OpenAI](https://openai.com/) on global frameworks for advanced AI access and compute governance.
- At the [Council on Foreign Relations](https://www.cfr.org/), I'm a graduate research assistant working on recursive self-improvement and international security with Adjunct Senior Fellow Gordon Goldstein.
- With [Doublethink Lab](https://doublethinklab.org/), I study foreign information manipulation and interference in the Taiwan Strait, including an October data sprint in Taipei.
- With the California Governor's Office of Emergency Services, I'm translating the state's SB 53 frontier AI framework into public-sector crisis management protocols and intervention thresholds.
- I'm co-mentoring a [SPAR](https://sparai.org/) project this fall, *Assessing Taiwan's Global Position for Transformative AI*, which is open to mentee applications through 18 August.

## What did I do before this?

- I spent several years as a data scientist and machine learning engineer working on AI in the U.S. national security community — AI assurance, including adversarial testing and alignment evaluation of Mandarin-language models, alongside production NLP and computer vision systems built in Python and PyTorch.
- I hold a B.A. with honors in Quantitative Social Science from Dartmouth College.
- I straddle geopolitical policy advising and full-stack technical work on AI. I work in Python, R, and SQL, with PyTorch and Hugging Face Transformers, and I read Chinese and speak Mandarin fluently.
